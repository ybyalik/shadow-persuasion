# Further Reading

These books and studies go deeper into ideas touched on in *Shadow Persuasion*.

## On influence and pressure

Brehm, Jack W. *A Theory of Psychological Reactance.* New York: Academic Press, 1966. The original work on why people push back when they feel pressured.

Cialdini, Robert B. *Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion.* New and expanded edition. New York: Harper Business, 2021.

Cialdini, Robert B. *Pre-Suasion: A Revolutionary Way to Influence and Persuade.* New York: Simon & Schuster, 2016.

Kahneman, Daniel. *Thinking, Fast and Slow.* New York: Farrar, Straus and Giroux, 2011.

Thorndike, Edward L. "A Constant Error in Psychological Ratings." *Journal of Applied Psychology* 4, no. 1 (1920): 25 to 29. The first description of the halo effect.

## On difficult conversations

Fisher, Roger, William Ury, and Bruce Patton. *Getting to Yes: Negotiating Agreement Without Giving In.* New York: Penguin.

Patterson, Kerry, Joseph Grenny, Ron McMillan, and Al Switzler. *Crucial Conversations: Tools for Talking When Stakes Are High.* New York: McGraw-Hill.

Stone, Douglas, Bruce Patton, and Sheila Heen. *Difficult Conversations: How to Discuss What Matters Most.* New York: Viking, 1999.

Voss, Chris, with Tahl Raz. *Never Split the Difference: Negotiating As If Your Life Depended On It.* New York: Harper Business, 2016.

## On manipulation and controlling relationships

Bancroft, Lundy. *Why Does He Do That? Inside the Minds of Angry and Controlling Men.* New York: Berkley Books, 2002.

Eddy, Bill. *BIFF: Quick Responses to High-Conflict People, Their Personal Attacks, Hostile Email and Social Media Meltdowns.* Scottsdale: Unhooked Books.

Stern, Robin. *The Gaslight Effect: How to Spot and Survive the Hidden Manipulation Others Use to Control Your Life.* New York: Morgan Road Books, 2007.
