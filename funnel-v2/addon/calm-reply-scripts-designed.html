How to use these scripts

Each script gives you a common line or situation, and a calm reply you can use as written or adapt to your own voice. They’re short on purpose. Long answers give people more to argue with. Short ones don’t.

Three rules make them work.

  1. Say it once, then stop. Don’t add reasons, apologies, or “but maybe.” The pause after a calm reply does half the work.
  2. If they push, repeat. You don’t need a new argument. The same sentence, in the same tone, is enough.
  3. Keep your voice low and slow. The words matter less than how steady you sound.

If someone is threatening you or you feel unsafe, these scripts aren’t the answer. Leave, and get support. In the US, the National Domestic Violence Hotline is 1-800-799-7233, or text START to 88788.

Part 1Saying no without excuses

1Someone asks for a favor you don’t want to do.

“Thanks for thinking of me, but I can’t do that.”

2You’re invited to something you don’t want to attend.

“I’m going to pass this time. I hope it’s a great night.”

3They ask why.

“It just doesn’t work for me.”

4They ask why again.

“I don’t have a better reason than that. It doesn’t work for me.”

5You need time before answering.

“Let me check and get back to you by tomorrow.”

6You want to help, but only a little.

“I can help for an hour on Saturday morning. That’s what I’ve got.”

7They try to negotiate you down.

“I understand. My answer’s still no.”

8You said yes earlier and need to take it back.

“I said yes too quickly. I’m not able to do it after all.”

9A recurring request you’re done with.

“I’ve been happy to do this in the past. I’m not going to keep doing it.”

10They say, “It’ll only take a minute.”

“Even so, I can’t take it on right now.”

Part 2Guilt trips

11“I’ll just do it myself, like always.”

“Okay. Let me know if you want help with something specific.”

12“After everything I’ve done for you…”

“You’ve done a lot for me, and I’m grateful. I’m still not able to do this.”

13“I thought I could count on you.”

“You can count on me for a lot. This one I can’t do.”

14“If you loved me, you’d do this.”

“I love you. I’m not going to do this.”

15“Fine. Don’t worry about me.”

“Okay. I’m around if you want to talk.”

16“A real friend would help.”

“I care about you, and I can’t help with this one.”

17“I guess I know where your priorities are.”

“I’m sorry you see it that way. My answer’s the same.”

18“You’d do it for anyone else.”

“Maybe. I’m still not doing it this time.”

19They remind you of a favor they did.

“I really appreciated that. It doesn’t change my answer on this.”

20A long sigh and silence after you decline.

“I can see you’re disappointed. I understand.” (Then stop.)

Part 3Gaslighting and rewriting the past

21“That never happened.”

“I remember it differently. Either way, here’s what I need going forward.”

22“I never said that.”

“That’s how I heard it. I’d like it not to happen again.”

23“You’re too sensitive.”

“Maybe. It still bothered me, and I’m asking you to stop.”

24“It was just a joke.”

“It didn’t land as a joke for me. Please don’t say it again.”

25“You’re overreacting.”

“I hear that you think so. I’m telling you how it affected me.”

26“You made me do it.”

“I’m responsible for what I did. You’re responsible for what you did.”

27“You agreed to this. Don’t you remember?”

“I don’t think I did. Either way, I’m not agreeing to it now.”

28“Everyone thinks you’re being unreasonable.”

“I’m not talking with everyone. I’m talking with you.”

29The conversation keeps going in circles.

“We’re going around in circles. Let’s come back to this tomorrow.”

30They change the subject to something you did.

“We can talk about that another time. Right now I’m asking about this.”

Part 4The silent treatment and withdrawal

31They stop speaking to you after a disagreement.

“I can tell you’re upset. When you’re ready to talk, I’m here.”

32“I’m fine.” (They’re clearly not.)

“Okay. If that changes, let me know.”

33They’ve gone cold for days.

“You seem distant lately. Is something going on between us?”

34Raising the pattern at a calm moment.

“When we disagree, you sometimes stop talking for a day or two. I’d rather we talk about it, even if it’s hard.”

35Affection is withdrawn until you apologize.

“I don’t want our closeness to depend on me always agreeing with you.”

36A friend stops replying after you said no.

“I’ve missed hearing from you. If something’s bothering you, I’m open to talking.”

37A coworker stops including you.

“I noticed I wasn’t in the planning meetings this month. How can I stay involved?”

38A slow reply that seems pointed.

“Just checking you saw my message about Saturday.”

39They expect you to guess what’s wrong.

“I’d like to help, but I can’t guess. You’ll need to tell me.”

40They come back as if nothing happened.

“I’m glad we’re talking again. I’d like to understand what happened last week.”

Part 5Flattery, charm, and fake urgency

41“You’re the only one I trust with this.”

“That’s kind of you. I can’t take it on this week.”

42“You’re so good at this. Would you mind…?”

“Thank you. Let me look at my week and get back to you.”

43“I need an answer right now.”

“If it has to be decided right now, then my answer is no.”

44“This offer ends tonight.”

“Then I’ll pass. I don’t make decisions like this on the same day.”

45A new partner is moving very fast.

“I really like spending time with you. I’d like to take things a bit slower.”

46“Once this is done, I’ll make it up to you.”

“That would be great. Can we agree on when, specifically?”

47They promise a reward that keeps not arriving.

“You’ve mentioned that a few times. I’d like to see it happen before I agree to more.”

48“I’ve never told anyone this, but…” followed by a request.

“Thank you for trusting me with that. I can’t help with this, though.”

49A small request starts growing.

“I said I’d take a quick look. I can’t take on the rest.”

50A salesperson keeps pushing.

“I appreciate your time. I’m not buying today.”

Part 6Work

51Your manager drops extra work on you.

“I’m at capacity. Which of my current projects should I move down the list?”

52A coworker asks you to cover again.

“I’ve covered a few times this month. I can’t do it again. Have you asked the team lead?”

53Someone repeats your idea and gets the credit.

“Thanks for picking up on the idea I raised earlier. Let me walk everyone through it.”

54Asking for a raise meeting.

“I’d like to set up thirty minutes to talk about my compensation.”

55“Not right now” on a raise.

“I understand. What would I need to show for it to happen at my next review? Can we put that in writing?”

56A weekend request.

“I’m not available this weekend. I can have it done by Tuesday.”

57A hint that your review could suffer.

“I want to make sure I understand. Are you saying my review will be affected if I don’t do this?”

58Your role has quietly grown.

“My role has grown quite a bit. I’d like to talk about adjusting either my responsibilities or my title and pay.”

59Confirming an agreement afterward. (By email.)

“Thanks for today. Just confirming that we agreed…”

60Criticism in front of the team.

“I’d like to talk about that one-on-one. Can we find time after the meeting?”

Part 7Money, loans, and favors

61A request to borrow money.

“I’m not able to lend money right now.”

62Following up on a loan.

“I wanted to check on the money from January. When can you get that back to me?”

63More requests while an old loan is unpaid.

“I’m not able to lend more until the earlier loan is sorted out.”

64Borrowed belongings never returned.

“Can you bring back the ladder this weekend? I need it.”

65“Temporary” that never ended.

“When we started, we said a few weeks. It’s been five months. I’d like it to end by the first of next month.”

66Setting an end date at the start.

“I’m happy to help until the end of March. Then we’ll check in.”

67Splitting costs unfairly.

“I’d like us to split this evenly from now on.”

68A gift with strings attached.

“That was really kind of you. It doesn’t change my answer on this.”

69A refund or replacement.

“I’d be happy with either a full refund or a replacement this week. Which works better for you?”

70A wrong charge.

“I was charged twice for this. Could you refund the second charge, please?”

Part 8Family and holidays

71“You never visit.”

“I can’t come every week, but I’d love to call every Sunday and visit once a month.”

72Holiday pressure.

“We’re spending this holiday with my side. We’d love to see you the weekend after.”

73A private topic raised at dinner.

“I’m not going to discuss that here.” (Then change the subject.)

74Unsolicited parenting advice.

“Thanks. We’ve decided to do it differently.”

75You’re expected to handle a parent’s care alone.

“I’m happy to keep doing the appointments. I need someone else to take the bills and pharmacy.”

76The unspoken family rule.

“I think there’s an assumption that I handle this because I live closest. I’d like to talk about whether that’s fair.”

77Being pulled into someone else’s fight.

“I’m not going to get involved in this one. I love you both.”

78Criticism of your choices.

“I know you’d have chosen differently. This is my decision.”

79“Your sister would do it.”

“That’s up to her. I’m not able to.”

80“You’ve changed.”

“I have, a bit.” (No apology needed.)

Part 9Anger, pressure, and public put-downs

81They raise their voice.

“I want to hear what you’re saying. I can’t do that while you’re shouting.”

82It keeps escalating.

“I’m going to step out. We can pick this up tomorrow.”

83An ultimatum.

“I’m not going to make a decision like that under pressure. I need some time.”

84A put-down in front of friends.

“That wasn’t funny to me.” (Calm, then move on.)

85Pressure in front of a group.

“I’ll think about it and let you know tomorrow.”

86“You’re being difficult.”

“I’m not being difficult. I’m being clear about what I can do.”

87A vague threat.

“What exactly do you mean by that?”

88They demand you apologize for something you didn’t do.

“I’m sorry this has been hard. I’m not going to apologize for something I didn’t do.”

89They interrupt you repeatedly.

“I’d like to finish my point, and then I want to hear yours.”

90After an angry outburst passes.

“I can see how frustrated you were. When you’re ready, I’d like to understand what’s behind it.”

Part 10Text-message versions

Short replies for texts and messages. Send once, then put the phone down.

91A favor request.

“Can’t this time. Hope it works out.”

92A last-minute ask.

“I’ll check and let you know tonight.”

93A guilt text.

“I know you were hoping I’d say yes. I can’t.”

94A pushy follow-up.

“My answer’s the same. Hope you understand.”

95An angry message.

“I’m not going to discuss this over text. Happy to talk when we’re both calm.”

96A message rewriting what happened.

“We remember it differently. Here’s what I’d like going forward: …”

97“Why aren’t you answering?”

“I’ve been busy. Here’s my answer now.”

98An ex reopening old arguments.

“I’m happy to talk about the schedule. I’m not discussing the rest.”

99A loan reminder.

“Hi, checking on the money from last month. When can you send it?”

100Ending a conversation that’s going nowhere.

“I’ve said what I needed to. Let’s leave it there for now.”
